A pre-launch is the one moment in a real estate project when nobody knows the price, the floor plans are still being finalised and the sales team needs a full diary of serious buyers anyway. Get it right and the project is half sold before the launch event. Get it wrong and you spend the budget collecting names nobody can call.

I lead digital marketing for a premium Delhi NCR real estate developer, with projects in Noida, Ghaziabad, Gurgaon and Delhi and tickets from ₹2.5 Crore to ₹30 Crore. This is how I planned the digital side of a 4 and 5 BHK luxury pre-launch in Noida.

## Step 1: Agree what "qualified" means

Before a single rupee went out, sales and marketing agreed on one definition of a qualified lead: right budget, real intent to buy in the coming months, and reachable. Everything else in the plan is judged against that number. Total leads, clicks and cost per lead are useful signals, but at luxury prices they can look great while the sales team has nobody worth calling.

## Step 2: Evaluate the platforms before spending

I looked at 16 platforms. For each one I asked three questions:

1. **Are buyers at this ticket size actually there?** A 4 BHK buyer in Noida is a different person from a first-home buyer.
2. **Can it be measured?** If a platform cannot show which leads turned into site visits, it gets a small test budget or nothing.
3. **What does it add that Meta and Google do not?** Reach into a new audience, a premium context or intent we cannot get elsewhere.

| Layer | Role in the plan | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Volume and intent, every day | Meta, Google Search |
| Reach | Premium audiences outside social | Programmatic (DV360), Taboola |
| Partners | Buyers already shopping for property | Property portals such as MagicBricks |
| Premium context | Seen by people who fit the ticket size | Uber Ads |

Most of the 16 did not make the cut. That is the point of evaluating first.

## Step 3: Launch on the base, add channels in steps

Meta and Google went live first because they give fast, measurable feedback. Programmatic and partner channels were onboarded next, each with its own tracking so their leads could be compared fairly against the base. A new channel earns more budget only when its qualified leads hold up.

## Step 4: Measure qualified leads, weekly

Every week the question was simple: where did qualified leads come from, and what did each one cost? Money moved toward channels producing buyers sales wanted to call, and away from channels producing volume.

## The result

In its first month, the launch delivered **108 of 168 qualified leads**. Alongside it, across the account, Meta lead qualification rose to 17.9% against a 5.9% account average, and Meta cost per lead fell 15.7% in a month. I wrote about that separately in [lifting Meta lead quality for real estate](/blog/meta-ads-lead-quality-real-estate/).

## What I would tell any developer planning a pre-launch

- **Decide the qualified lead definition with sales first.** It changes every decision after it.
- **Evaluate widely, launch narrowly.** Sixteen platforms looked at, a handful used.
- **Give every channel its own tracking.** Otherwise the loudest channel takes credit for everything.
- **Review weekly.** A pre-launch moves too fast for monthly reports.
