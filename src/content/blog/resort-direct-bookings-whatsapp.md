Most resorts I see in India pay a booking site commission on most of their rooms, then spend on ads that send people to that same booking site. It works, in the sense that rooms fill. But every booking pays the platform, and the resort never owns the guest's phone number or their next visit.

Between August 2025 and February 2026 I ran paid media and automation for Machan Corbett Resort and Earavat Resort in Jim Corbett. The brief was simple: more bookings that come straight to the resort.

## The results first

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Confirmed direct bookings | ₹1.4 Crore+ |
| Total ad spend | ₹1,20,000 |
| Return on ad spend | 116x |
| Direct share of bookings | 18% → 41% |
| WhatsApp chat to booking | 22% (benchmark 4 to 8%) |

## 1. Send the traffic to WhatsApp, not to a booking site

The biggest single change was where the ads pointed. Instead of a booking site listing, ads opened a WhatsApp conversation with the resort. Indian guests already plan trips on WhatsApp: they want to ask about the pool, the food, the safari timings, whether the room fits a family of five. A booking site can't answer those questions. A person, or a well-built concierge, can.

That one change is what moved the direct share of bookings from 18% to 41%.

## 2. One campaign per kind of guest

A resort does not have one customer. These two sell four very different things, so each got its own campaign, its own creative and its own message:

- **Destination weddings**, with an average ticket of ₹18 Lakh. A long decision made by families, about dates, capacity, food and the look of the venue.
- **Corporate retreats**, with an average group booking of ₹6 Lakh. Decided by HR or a founder, about dates, rooms for the whole team, and space for sessions.
- **Leisure stays.** Couples and families deciding quickly, about price, the pool and the food.
- **Wildlife packages.** Planned around safari timing, and they sold out the quarterly allocation.

Mixing these in one campaign would have let the cheapest clicks win, and the cheapest clicks are almost never the wedding.

## 3. A WhatsApp concierge that answers in seconds

Every chat gets the same start, at any hour:

1. An instant reply with the right brochure for what they asked about.
2. A few questions to understand the booking: dates, number of guests, the occasion.
3. A handover to the team with everything already collected.

That flow converted **22% of chats into bookings**, against a 4 to 8% hospitality benchmark. Most of that gap is speed. A guest comparing three resorts on a Friday night books the one that replies first with a clear answer.

## 4. A website built for search, not just for looks

Ads stop when the budget stops. To give Earavat a source of bookings that doesn't depend on ads, I built a new 14 page website with one page for each thing people search for (weddings, retreats, rooms, the location and so on). It has structured data, an llms.txt file and answer-first FAQs, so Google and AI assistants can read it, and it scores 95 on mobile Lighthouse.

## What any resort can take from this

- **Own the conversation.** A WhatsApp number on every ad, every listing and every brochure.
- **Split your guests.** Weddings, groups and couples need different ads and different answers.
- **Reply instantly.** Speed beats a discount more often than you'd expect.
- **Measure direct share, not just occupancy.** Full rooms that all paid commission are a weaker business than slightly fewer rooms booked direct.
