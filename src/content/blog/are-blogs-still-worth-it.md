Every client meeting this year has had a version of the same question: if ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews answer people directly, why are we still paying for blogs?

It is a fair question. A lot of blogs really are dead weight now. But the answer is not "stop blogging". It is "stop writing the posts AI can write without you".

## What AI answers actually replaced

Search for "what is a lead magnet" and you get a summary at the top that is as good as most of the articles below it. Those articles were written from the same handful of sources, so the AI can summarise them all at once. Nobody needs to click.

That is the kind of post that lost its value: the generic explainer, written to a keyword, with nothing in it that came from the writer's own work.

## What AI answers still need

AI tools cannot invent first-hand experience. They need someone to have run the campaign, measured the result and written it down. Search for "cost per lead for Maldives travel ads in India" and there is no textbook answer. There are only people who have spent the money.

Google's own guidance points the same way. Its [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says there are no special requirements or special schema for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode: the page has to be indexed and eligible for a snippet, and normal SEO practice applies. Its [helpful content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks whether content offers original information, reporting, research or analysis, and whether it shows first-hand experience. Neither rewards volume.

## What it looked like across 16 websites

At my day job I look after SEO, AEO and GEO for 16 real estate websites. We stopped publishing generic articles and focused on pages with specific, answerable questions, clear structure and facts only we could give: project details, locations, configurations, prices and processes. In one month:

| Measure | Change in one month |
|---|---|
| Organic leads | +19.3% |
| Branded clicks | +33.4% |
| ChatGPT referrals | +18% |
| Organic leads that qualify | up to 45% |

The last row is the reason to keep going. People who arrive from a page that answered their question properly are already informed, so a far higher share of them are real buyers.

## The test I use before writing any post

1. **Could someone write this without having done the work?** If yes, an AI answer already covers it. Skip it or add your own data.
2. **Is there a number or example only we have?** That is what gets cited.
3. **Does it answer one question clearly in the first few sentences?** That is what gets quoted.
4. **Would the reader trust who wrote it?** A named author with visible experience, not "admin".
5. **Does it link to the proof?** A case study, a screenshot, a project page.

## What I would stop, start and keep

| Stop | Start | Keep |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword-stuffed explainers | Posts built on your own numbers | Answer-first openings |
| Publishing to a fixed word count | Comparisons from real campaigns | FAQs with direct answers |
| Rewriting competitors' articles | Case studies with the method explained | Internal links between related posts |
| Anonymous authors | Author pages with real background | Fast, crawlable pages |

## So, are blogs worth it?

For a business with real experience to share, more than before, because generic competition is being filtered out. For a business that only republishes what everyone else says, no, and AI answers have made that obvious.
