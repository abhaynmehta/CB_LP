Ask a real estate marketing team how a campaign is doing and you will usually hear a cost per lead. Ask the sales head the same question and you will hear how many families came to the site this week. Only one of those numbers pays for the project, and it is not the first one.

## Why cost per lead misleads in real estate

At luxury ticket sizes a lead is a name and a phone number, nothing more. Some of those people will buy. Most will not answer, will not have the budget, or filled the form while scrolling. Cost per lead rewards whichever campaign collects names most cheaply, and the cheapest names are often the least likely to visit.

Here is the arithmetic that changes the conversation:

| | Campaign A | Campaign B |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead | ₹200 | ₹500 |
| Lead to site visit | 5% | 25% |
| Cost per site visit | ₹4,000 | ₹2,000 |

Campaign A wins the cost-per-lead report and loses the business. **Cost per site visit**, or simply lead to site visit next to cost per lead, is the pair that shows the truth. (The figures in this table are an illustration of the maths, not client data.)

## How to measure it properly

1. **Count visits done, not visits scheduled.** A scheduled visit that never happens is a lead, not a visit.
2. **Use the lead date as the anchor.** Measure what share of leads that arrived in a given week went on to visit. That way slow follow-up shows up as a lower number instead of hiding.
3. **Split by source and campaign.** Meta lead forms, Meta WhatsApp ads, Google Search, portals and walk-ins behave very differently. One blended number hides which channel is carrying the others.
4. **Give it time.** Luxury buyers often visit two to four weeks after enquiring. Read the number for leads that are at least a few weeks old.

## What moved it from 8% to 44%

Between March and June 2026 I managed 12+ real estate accounts at once in NCR, with more than ₹30 Lakh of spend across 50+ campaigns in three months. Against an 8% industry average, the accounts reached **44% lead to site visit**, and together they built **₹22.4 Crore+ of qualified sales pipeline in 90 days**. Four changes did most of the work.

**Instant first contact.** Every lead got a WhatsApp reply within seconds, with the brochure and a few questions. A buyer who hears back in minutes remembers the project; one who hears back the next afternoon has often booked visits elsewhere. The full flow is in [hot, warm, cold WhatsApp qualification](/blog/real-estate-lead-qualification-whatsapp/).

**Qualification before the call.** Budget, configuration and timeline were asked up front, and each lead was tagged hot, warm or cold. Salespeople called hot leads the same day instead of working through the list in order.

**Better leads at the top.** Showing the configuration and starting price in the ad filtered out people who were never going to buy at that ticket, and sending qualified leads back to Meta taught campaigns who to find. I wrote about that signal in [the Conversions API for real estate](/blog/meta-conversions-api-real-estate/).

**A weekly review with sales.** Every week: which sources produced visits, which produced nothing, and why the non-visitors did not come. Budget moved on that, not on cost per lead.

## The takeaway

Cost per lead is a useful health check for the ads. Lead to site visit is the number the business runs on. Track both, judge campaigns on the second, and fix follow-up speed before you spend another rupee on more leads.
