Every real estate marketer knows the pattern. Meta sends a flood of leads, sales calls them, and most say they never filled a form, cannot afford the project or were just curious. The cost per lead looks fine in the report and terrible in the sales meeting.

At a premium Delhi NCR developer, with luxury projects in Noida, Ghaziabad, Gurgaon and Delhi and tickets from ₹2.5 Crore to ₹30 Crore, Meta lead qualification sat at a 5.9% account average. Within a month it reached **17.9%**, and Meta cost per lead fell **15.7%** at the same time.

| Measure | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Meta lead qualification | 5.9% (account average) | 17.9% |
| Meta cost per lead | baseline | 15.7% lower in a month |

I will not share the exact audiences or campaign structures, but the principles travel to any real estate account.

## 1. Teach Meta what a good lead is

Meta learns from the signal you send it. If the only signal is "form submitted", it gets better at finding people who submit forms. When qualified leads and site visits are sent back through the Conversions API and offline conversions, Meta starts finding people who look like real buyers. This was the single biggest lever.

## 2. Filter before the form, not after it

The ad and the form should make the price level obvious. Showing the configuration and the starting price in the creative turns away people who were never going to buy at that ticket. Fewer leads, but far more that sales wants.

## 3. Judge ads on qualified leads, not cost per lead

Some ads produced cheap leads that never qualified. Others cost more per lead and qualified well. Once every ad was judged on cost per qualified lead, budget moved quickly, and that is also why the overall cost per lead fell.

## 4. Close the loop with sales every week

Lead quality is a sales number, so it needs sales feedback. A short weekly review of which leads qualified, and why the others did not, fed straight back into creative and targeting decisions.

## The takeaway

Cheap leads are easy. Qualified leads come from sending the right signal back to the platform, filtering early and measuring what sales cares about.
