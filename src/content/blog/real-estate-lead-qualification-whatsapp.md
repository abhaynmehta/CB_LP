Real estate is the easiest place to get leads and the hardest place to get buyers. A Meta lead form can bring in hundreds of names a week. Most of them are curious, early or simply the wrong budget, and a sales team that calls every one of them in order burns out on the wrong people while the real buyers wait.

Between March and June 2026 I managed 12+ real estate accounts at once for Lucentia Digital in NCR, with more than ₹30 Lakh of spend across 50+ campaigns in three months. The single change that moved results most was not in the ads. It was what happened in the first five minutes after someone filled the form.

## The problem with "call every lead"

Three things go wrong when every lead lands in a sheet and waits for a call:

- **Speed.** People enquire at night and on weekends. By the time someone calls the next afternoon, they have looked at three other projects.
- **Order.** Calls go in the order leads arrive, not in the order of how ready people are to buy.
- **Memory.** Someone who is six months away from buying gets one call, says "later" and is never contacted again.

## What the WhatsApp bot does

I built Wati WhatsApp flows for 6+ projects. Every lead goes through the same short path:

1. **An instant reply on WhatsApp**, at any hour, with the project brochure attached.
2. **A few qualifying questions**: budget, the size of home they want, and when they plan to buy.
3. **A tag** based on the answers: hot, warm or cold.
4. **A different next step for each tag.**

| Tag | Who it is | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| Hot | Right budget, buying soon | A sales call the same day |
| Warm | Interested, not urgent | Nurture messages after 1 day and 3 days of silence |
| Cold | Browsing or wrong fit | The brochure and occasional updates |

The point is not automation for its own sake. It is that the sales team's first call goes to the person most likely to visit the site, and nobody who showed interest is forgotten.

## The results

Across the accounts I managed:

- **44% lead to site visit**, against an industry average of about 8%.
- **₹22.4 Crore+ of qualified sales pipeline** in a 90 day period.

A site visit is the number that matters in real estate. Nobody books a ₹3 Crore apartment from an ad; they book it after standing in the show flat.

## Close the loop with Meta

The bot fixes what happens after the lead. The other half is teaching the ad platform which leads were good. I set up the **Meta Conversions API** with **offline conversion tracking**, so qualified leads and site visits were sent back to Meta, and built **lookalike audiences from verified buyer lists**. Instead of optimising for anyone who fills a form, the campaigns learn to find people who look like actual buyers.

Without this, Meta keeps finding more of the cheapest leads, because cheap form fills are the only signal it gets.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Too many questions.** Every question you add loses people. Ask what decides the next step and leave the rest for the call.
- **No human handover.** A hot lead stuck talking to a bot is a lost sale. Route it to a person immediately.
- **Nurture that sounds like spam.** Warm leads need something useful: a price update, a floor plan, a site visit slot, not "Hi, any update?"
- **Tags nobody uses.** If sales still calls the sheet top to bottom, the tagging was wasted. Agree on the rules with the sales team first.
