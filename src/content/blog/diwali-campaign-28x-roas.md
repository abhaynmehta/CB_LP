Festive campaigns have a reputation for being easy money: everyone is buying, so any ad works. In my experience the opposite is true. Every brand is advertising at the same time, costs rise, and the brands that win are the ones that did the work before the festival started.

From July 2024 to January 2025 I ran social and paid growth for Dohful, a premium handcrafted cookie brand. The Diwali campaign is the part people ask about.

## The numbers

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Ad spend | ₹45,000 |
| Revenue | ₹13.2 Lakh |
| Time | 28 days |
| Return on ad spend | 28.9x |
| Diwali buyers who reordered within 45 days | 28% |
| Instagram followers | 22K → 27K |
| Views in a month | 2.24 million |

## 1. Build the brand before you buy the traffic

The Diwali ads did not start from zero. In the months before, I worked on Dohful's brand identity and storytelling formats and on an Instagram strategy built for engagement. By the time the festive campaign ran, the account had grown from 22K to 27K followers and pulled 2.24 million views in a month.

That matters because people check. Someone who sees an ad for a gift box taps the profile before they buy. A profile full of real posts, comments and faces makes the decision easy. A dead profile makes the ad look like a risk.

## 2. Sell the gift, not the product

At Diwali nobody needs cookies. They need a gift that looks thoughtful, arrives on time and doesn't feel like another box of dry fruits. So the ads sold the gift: the box, the moment of giving, and the founder holding the gift box. A real person behind a small brand is more convincing than a perfect product photo.

## 3. Keep the spend tight

₹45,000 is a small budget for a festive season, and that was the point. A small, focused budget forced every rupee to go to the creative and the audience that were already working, instead of spreading across every idea at once.

## 4. Plan for the second order

The number I'm proudest of isn't the ROAS. It's that **28% of Diwali buyers reordered within 45 days**. The first order is paid for by ads. The second one usually isn't, and that is where a festive spike turns into a business. Good product and a reason to come back (the storytelling, the brand, the follow-up) did that.

## The ROAS math, simply

Return on ad spend is revenue divided by ad spend. Whether a ROAS is good depends on margin:

- **Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin.** At a 40% margin, you need 2.5x just to pay for the ads.
- Anything above that is profit before other costs.

28.9x is far above break-even for any food brand. It is also not normal, so don't plan a budget around it. It happened because the brand, the offer, the creative and the timing lined up in one season.

## What to copy for your next festival

- Start building content and audience weeks before the festival, not on day one.
- Sell the occasion and the person, not just the product.
- Put a small budget behind what already works before testing new ideas.
- Measure repeat purchase, not just the first sale.
