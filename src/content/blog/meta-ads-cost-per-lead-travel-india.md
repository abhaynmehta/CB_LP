When a tour operator asks me "what should a lead cost?", the honest answer is that almost nobody publishes real numbers. Search for it and you find tool pages with India-wide averages, vendor blogs quoting anywhere from ₹100 to ₹800, and case studies that hide the absolute figures. So here are mine, from running Meta Ads for 12+ tour operators between 2024 and 2026, including WanderLynk, WanderMesh, Canopy & Sky and The Travel Masters.

## The numbers

All of these are Indian audiences, buying international trips.

| Destination or format | Result | Cost per result |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnam | 1,025 leads | ₹44.87 |
| Bali | 1,471 leads | ₹50 |
| Maldives | 183 leads | ₹96 (about $1) |
| Group trips | lead forms | ₹37 |
| One campaign, lead forms | 3,853 leads | ₹36.67 |
| One campaign, WhatsApp | 10,694 chats | ₹4.62 |

Together these campaigns brought in more than **10,500 international trip enquiries** across Bali, the Maldives, Mauritius, Thailand, Vietnam and Dubai.

## Why the Maldives costs double Bali, and why that's fine

The Maldives lead costs about twice a Bali lead. That is expected. A Maldives trip is a bigger purchase, the audience that can afford it is smaller, and Meta charges more to reach it. Comparing destinations on cost per lead alone would push you to sell only cheap trips. The fair comparison is **cost per booking against the margin on that booking**: a ₹96 lead that becomes a premium Maldives package can be worth far more than a ₹50 Bali lead.

## A cheap lead is not a good lead

The most common trap I see is a campaign that celebrates a low cost per lead while sales complains every lead is junk. The warning signs I watch for:

- **Cheap leads that never convert.** The ad is attracting people who like the photo, not people planning a trip.
- **Low click-through rate.** The creative or the offer is not landing, and you are paying to show it anyway.
- **High cost and weak quality together.** Fix the creative and the targeting before you add budget. More money only scales the problem.

A WhatsApp chat at ₹4.62 and a form lead at ₹50 are not the same thing. A chat is the start of a conversation, so count what happens next: quotes sent, calls taken and trips booked.

## What actually moved the numbers

None of this came from a secret setting. It came from doing the basics every time:

1. **Lead with the price.** A hook like "Bali from ₹X per person" filters out people who were never going to buy and pulls in the ones who were.
2. **Show what's included.** A short checklist of flights, stay, transfers and sightseeing answers the first three questions before anyone asks them.
3. **Give a one-word next step.** "DM BALI" or a WhatsApp button turns interest into a conversation in one tap.
4. **Make every ad in two shapes.** Square for the feed and vertical for stories and reels. Half the inventory is vertical.
5. **Send traffic somewhere that sells.** I built a landing website for the operators I worked with, so the ad and the page told the same story.
6. **Verify the lead.** Lead forms with phone verification cost a little more per lead and save the sales team hours of chasing wrong numbers.

## Spend when Indians actually travel

Indian demand runs on its own calendar:

- **Honeymoons** peak after the wedding season, roughly December to February.
- **The Maldives** dry season runs December to April.
- **Families** book around the May and June school holidays.
- **Christmas to New Year** lifts every destination.

Southeast Asia (Thailand, Vietnam, Bali) wins on price. Europe, Japan and Australia lose some buyers on cost and visas, so expect higher costs per lead there.

## How to use these benchmarks

Treat my numbers as a reference point, not a target. They are Indian audiences, 2024 to 2026, on accounts I set up and ran. Your offer, your creative and your follow-up speed will move them. Run your own campaign for two to three weeks, then compare like with like: forms with forms, chats with chats, and always the cost per booking at the end.
